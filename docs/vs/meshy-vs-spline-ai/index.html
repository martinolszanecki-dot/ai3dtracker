Meshy vs Spline AI

Short answer: pick Meshy if you want a dedicated AI 3D generator: a free plan, a predictable credit cost per model, a long export list (FBX, OBJ, USDZ, GLB, STL, BLEND), rigging and animation. Pick Spline AI if your real goal is an interactive 3D scene for the web and you want to generate objects right inside that editor. Fun fact: Meshy is one of the engines you can pick inside Spline.

  • Models: Meshy runs its own Meshy 7.1 (Meshy 6 and 6 Lite still offered, plus the Meshy T2.5 Smart Topology model). Spline doesn't list a 3D model of its own: per its docs you choose an engine per run: Hunyuan3D (default), Hunyuan 3.1 Pro, Trellis 2 or Meshy.
  • Price: Meshy Pro is $20/mo for 1,000 credits (up to 100 assets, ≈$0.20 each by Meshy's figure; a textured 7.1 model costs 30 credits). Spline's AI generation starts on Hobby at $15/seat/mo ($12 billed yearly) with 2,000 AI credits, but Spline publishes no fixed cost per model: credits go on both the image previews and the mesh, and an estimate is shown before each run.
  • Free use: Meshy Free gives 100 credits/month and free-plan assets can be used commercially under CC BY 4.0 (credit Meshy). Spline's free plan has no AI credits at all.
  • Export & printing: Meshy exports STL on its plans and is aimed at print hobbyists too. Spline exports GLTF with textures, USDZ and STL only on Pro ($30/seat/mo) and up; its strength is web embeds and code export.
  • License & data: paid Meshy users own their assets. Spline says you keep ownership of your content, but its updated Terms (effective Oct 29, 2026) let it use de-identified content, prompts and AI interactions for any commercial purpose, including training AI models.

Based on official pricing pages, docs and terms checked 2026-11-02. We have not run a blind quality test; output quality in Spline depends on which engine you pick.
