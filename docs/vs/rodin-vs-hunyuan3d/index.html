Rodin (Hyper3D) vs Hunyuan3D (Tencent)

Short answer: pick Rodin (Hyper3D) if you want a polished creator app with pay-by-result previews, high-res 8K/12K textures and, since November, prompt-editable auto-rigging, and you're fine paying a subscription for exports and commercial use. Pick Hunyuan3D if you want the lowest cost per model through Tencent's pay-as-you-go API, a free daily quota on the hosted platform, or open weights you can run yourself.

  • Models: Rodin runs Gen-2.5 (Agent-Ready update Sep 13, 2026; Gen-3 teased but not released). Tencent hosts Hunyuan3D 3.1 (up to 8 input views), though the global Pro API still defaults to 3.0; the newest open-weight release is 2.1.
  • Price: Rodin Creator is $30/mo ($24 billed yearly) for about 60 models (≈$0.50 each by Hyper3D's estimate); Business is $120/mo for about 416 models (≈$0.29) and is the plan with API access. Hunyuan3D's API has no subscription: credit packs from $15 per 1,000 credits, and a textured Professional generation costs 25 credits (≈$0.34–0.375; ≈$0.50 postpaid).
  • Free use: Rodin lets you generate for free and pay only when you confirm a result, but free exports are limited to legacy models. Tencent lists 20 free generations a day on its global platform and gives 200 one-time API trial credits.
  • Rigging & topology: Rodin added auto-rigging on Oct 23 and made it prompt-editable on Nov 3 (e.g. "Add tail bones", finger-level rigs, motion presets); it also has Smart Low-Poly and High-Poly Quads (Business). Hunyuan3D offers auto-rigging (10 credits), smart topology and a LowPoly mode in the API.
  • Formats & printing: both export GLB, FBX, OBJ, USDZ and STL.
  • License: Rodin paid plans include unlimited export and any use. Hosted Hunyuan3D follows Tencent's terms; the open-weight 2.1 license does not apply in the EU, UK or South Korea and needs a separate license above 1M monthly users.

Based on official pricing pages, changelogs and docs checked 2026-11-09 (Tencent's 20 free generations/day comes from its launch press release and sits behind a login). We have not run a blind quality test of the two models.
